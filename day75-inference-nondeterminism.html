Who else was in your batch

The kernel is not told which split to use. It is given a cost model — the reduction work per block, how many blocks the machine runs at once, and what an extra split costs in the epilogue — and it picks the K that finishes soonest. That choice is a function of B, the number of rows in the launch, which is a function of who else was being served. Times are in the model’s own units; the chosen split is in bold.

Which split is the accurate one

Every split reduced from the same products on the same states, so this comparison is paired — the only thing that changes between rows is the order of the additions. mean |Δ| is the average distance from the exact logit, measured in units of one top-two gap.

Where in the reply it differs — and whether anyone notices

A flip is absorbing: once one token changes, the state that produced the next one has changed too, so everything after it is a different continuation. The closed form uses the measured per-token flip rate and the measured rate at which two decorrelated continuations happen to agree; it is a prediction of the shape, not a fit to it.

The flakiest test in your suite is the longest one

An exact-match assertion over a generated reply fails when any token in it moves. That is a function of how many tokens the test asks for and nothing else — not of what it tests, not of how hard it is.

The accumulator width is a dial, not a fix

Widening the accumulator halves the error per bit and it never reaches zero — except at the far end of this construction, where the terms are exact integers and a wide enough accumulator is exactly right. That last part is a property of the toy and is declared rather than used.

A width is usually only known to a range — nobody publishes which reductions in a serving stack run in which precision. The flip rate a range delivers is then an integral of the law over that range, not a point evaluation of it: a composite-Simpson average against the closed form.

The gap is the whole story

Every token’s logit reads the same state, so the dot products are large and nearly equal and the decision lives in their difference. ρ is that ratio: the size of a logit divided by the size of the part that differs between tokens. It is the only structural parameter on this page, and the error the accumulator makes is set by the first number while the decision is set by the second.

Turning the temperature up, with the seed nailed down

At τ=0 a perturbation has to cross exactly one boundary — the top-two gap. At τ>0 the draw lands somewhere in a cumulative distribution with V−1 boundaries, and every one of them moved.

The control

What is actually being measured

Every quantity on this page comes out of four declared objects and nothing else.

  • A model is a weight matrix of integers. Row j is a shared vector C plus a deviation of size A/ρ. That is the structural fact everything rests on: all V rows read the same state, so the dot products are large and nearly equal and the winner is decided by the small part. A real language model behaves this way for the same reason — one residual stream, one unembedding matrix — and ρ is swept below rather than assumed.
  • A logit is the dot product of the state with one row: d products, added up. The inputs are integers small enough that every product is exact in a double and the total fits one, so the exactly correct logit is computable and is not a higher-precision estimate. It is checked against a BigInt sum of the same terms.
  • A reduction is an order. split-k cuts the d terms into K contiguous chunks, reduces each, then adds the partials. K=1 is plain left-to-right accumulation, and the page asserts the two are identical bit for bit rather than assuming it. The accumulator holds p significant bits and rounds half-to-even, which is what a p-bit significand does — not a model of rounding, the rounding itself.
  • A decode takes the argmax (or samples), mixes the chosen token into the state by a declared integer mixing function, and repeats. The mixing function is not a transformer and does not pretend to be; its only job is to make a different token lead to a different continuation.

Where the nondeterminism actually enters

There are exactly three places a reply could change between two occasions, and the page separates them:

  • The sampler. Its draws come from a seeded stream. Fix the seed and this term is gone. At τ=0 it never existed, because the argmax does not read the stream at all.
  • The weights. They are identical by construction — the same model object serves both runs.
  • The reduction order. Chosen by the launch, which is sized by the traffic. Nothing in a request body reaches it. This is the only term left at τ=0 with a fixed seed, and the scoreboard is what it is worth.

Which is why the two rows at the top of the scoreboard are identical to the last digit: varying the seed cannot move a number that was never a function of it. The page asserts that as an identity over every column rather than eyeballing the table.

What went against the build

What this does not model

  • Attention, layer norm, MoE routing. A real forward pass has hundreds of reductions, not one, and several of them are order-sensitive in the same way. One reduction is the smallest object that shows the mechanism; more of them can only make the per-token flip rate larger.
  • A real vocabulary. V tokens here against a hundred thousand there. The page sweeps ρ instead, because the quantity that matters is the size of the top-two gap relative to the size of the logit, and that is what ρ sets.
  • Non-determinism inside a single kernel. Atomic accumulation orders vary run to run even at a fixed batch size on real hardware. That would add a term this page does not have, in the same direction.
  • The cost model. The throughput column is a declared model of one launch, not a measurement of a GPU. It decides which split the kernel picks and what pinning costs, and both of those are stated as model outputs. Everything to its left — every divergence, error and flip rate — is arithmetic and does not depend on it.
  • Exactness at the top of the precision sweep. The terms here are integers, so a wide enough accumulator is exactly right and divergence is exactly zero. In a real stack it would be very small and not zero. The transferable part is the halving law, not the floor.

Everything the page asserts about itself

Bounds and identities only. Nothing here compares a computed number against a constant that was typed in.
